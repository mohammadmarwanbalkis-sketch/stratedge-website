What should a feasibility study actually contain?

At minimum: a defensible estimate of demand, the competitive position you would be entering, the cost base required to serve it, and the conditions under which the venture fails. A study that only describes the market without stating what would make the investment unwise has not finished the job.

How do you size a market when published data is thin?

By triangulating. Top-down from whatever published figures exist, bottom-up from observable units — outlets, transactions, capacity, headcount — and laterally from comparable markets. Where the three disagree, the disagreement itself is informative and gets reported rather than averaged away.

When is research worth the cost?

When the decision it informs is larger than the research. If a study costs a fraction of the capital at risk and can change the decision, it pays for itself. If the decision is already made and the research is there to justify it, it is not worth commissioning.

Signals

  • The market size in the business plan came from a single published figure

What the work covers

Research engagements are scoped backwards from the decision. Before any fieldwork begins we establish what you are deciding, what evidence would change that decision, and what threshold of confidence is required. This is what separates useful research from an expensive document.

The work typically combines desk research on market structure and published data; competitor benchmarking on proposition, pricing and footprint; and primary research with customers, channel partners or industry participants. Where willingness to pay matters — and for most new propositions it is the assumption carrying the most risk — it is tested directly rather than inferred.

Findings are reported with their limitations attached. A number presented without its confidence interval or its source is not evidence, and treating it as such is how research misleads the people who commissioned it.

Research that changes decisions, not research that confirms them

The most common failure in commissioned research is not inaccuracy. It is irrelevance — a thorough report that never touched the assumption the decision actually rested on.

We work the other way round. The riskiest assumptions get identified first, and the research budget is spent disproportionately on those. A study that resolves the two assumptions capable of sinking the venture is worth more than one that describes twelve market segments in even detail.

Independence matters here in a practical sense: we have no product, platform or commission arrangement attached to the findings, so there is nothing for the research to conclude in favour of.
