Why has profitability stalled while revenue keeps growing?

Usually because the cost of serving each additional unit of revenue has risen without anyone noticing. Growth hides this: headcount is added to relieve pressure rather than to a designed structure, exceptions become routine, and the margin quietly erodes one workaround at a time.

Why does every decision still escalate to the owner?

Because authority was never explicitly delegated, only informally tolerated. In most owner-led businesses the constraint is not capability but the absence of a decision-rights framework — who decides what, within what limits, and what happens when they are exceeded.

What does an operating model review actually examine?

How work enters the business, moves through it and leaves; who owns each step; where work waits; what is measured and by whom; and how the structure either supports or fights that flow. The output is a redesign with an implementation sequence, not an organogram.

What the work covers

Operational work begins by observing the business as it actually runs, not as the process documentation describes it. The gap between the two is usually where the cost sits.

Process mapping traces the real path of work through the organisation and identifies where it waits, doubles back or depends on a single individual. Structural review examines whether the organisation's shape supports that flow or cuts across it. Accountability work makes decision rights explicit — a step that sounds administrative and routinely produces the largest change in pace. Cost base analysis separates the spend that produces margin from the spend that has simply accumulated.

The output is a redesign paired with a sequence, because operational change consumes management attention and attempting it all at once is the most reliable way to achieve none of it.

Why operational change usually fails

It fails when it is designed away from the people who do the work. Redesign produced in a meeting room, handed down as a new process, meets an organisation that knows why the old workaround existed — and quietly reverts.

It also fails when measurement is not changed alongside the process. People optimise for what is measured. Redesigning the workflow while leaving the old metrics in place produces the old behaviour in a new format.

We work alongside your team rather than around it for this reason, and every engagement closes with a performance framework your managers can run without us.
