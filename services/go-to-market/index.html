Why are we not winning work we should be winning?

Most often because the proposition is described in terms of what the business does rather than the problem the buyer is trying to solve, so the buyer cannot tell whether it applies to them. The second most common cause is being present in the wrong channel — visible to people who do not make the decision.

What does a go-to-market plan include?

Who specifically you are selling to, what you are claiming and why it is credible, through which channels, at what price, and how the sales process converts interest into commitment. A plan missing any of those five is a marketing plan rather than a go-to-market plan.

Is this branding or sales work?

Both, deliberately. Positioning that never reaches the sales conversation changes nothing, and a sales process built on an unclear proposition works harder than it needs to. The engagement covers the line from claim to conversion.

What the work covers

Positioning work establishes the ground you intend to own in the buyer's mind, and whether that ground is both credible for you and valuable to them. Claims that are true but unimportant, or important but not credible from you, are the two most common failure modes.

Proposition work translates that position into something specific per segment, because a single message pitched at everyone is usually compelling to nobody. Channel and partnership strategy establishes where those buyers actually are and how they prefer to buy. Sales enablement converts all of it into a process your team can run: what is said, in what order, and what evidence is produced at each stage.

Measurement is defined at the same time. A go-to-market plan without a defined measure of success cannot be corrected, only defended.

Positioning in a market where everyone claims the same thing

In professional and B2B markets in the region, most competing propositions are close to interchangeable on the surface — quality, expertise, partnership, results. Differentiation rarely comes from claiming a different adjective.

It comes from being specific: about who you are for, what you will not do, and what evidence supports the claim. Specificity costs some breadth of appeal, which is exactly why it works. The engagement is largely an exercise in deciding what to give up.
