What does entering the UAE market actually involve?

Four decisions, in order: whether the opportunity justifies entry at all, what commercial model to enter with, where and through whom to operate, and how the first year will be measured. Licensing and legal structure follow those decisions rather than leading them — a common and expensive inversion.

Should we enter through a partner, a franchise or our own operation?

It depends on how much control the proposition requires, how much capital you are willing to commit before validation, and whether the value sits in something a partner could replicate. The engagement works through those trade-offs against your specific offer rather than applying a default.

Mainland or free zone?

This is usually presented as a legal question and is mostly a commercial one: who your customers are, whether you need to trade directly within the local market, and what your cost and ownership structure needs to look like. We work through the commercial trade-offs; the licensing and legal execution should be confirmed with a licensed corporate services provider or legal counsel.

What the work covers

Entry engagements start with a question many advisers skip: whether to enter at all. An honest assessment sometimes concludes that the opportunity does not justify the commitment, or that the timing is wrong — and that finding, delivered before the capital is spent, is among the most valuable outcomes the work can produce.

Where entry is justified, the work defines the commercial model and the structure to support it, sets criteria for partner or channel selection rather than simply making introductions, and analyses location and footprint against where demand actually sits. It closes with a launch plan carrying milestones and a first-year performance framework.

Regulatory and licensing requirements change, and specialist providers exist for that execution. Our work is the commercial decision that determines which structure you should be asking them to set up.

Why entries underperform

The most common cause is a proposition transplanted unchanged. What differentiates a business in its home market frequently does not differentiate it here, because the competitive set, the price expectation and the buying process are different. Testing that assumption before launch is cheaper than discovering it after.

The second is underestimating the time to first revenue, and therefore the working capital required to reach it. Entry plans built on optimistic ramp assumptions run out of runway at precisely the point where the model was about to start working.

The third is partner selection made on availability rather than on criteria. A partner chosen because they were introduced first is a decision made by accident.
